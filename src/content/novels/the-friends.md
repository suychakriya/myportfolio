# I. Arc 1 — Jess

The cafeteria was noisy like always.
Jess sat across from Yunnie, stirring the melting ice in her lemonade even though it didn’t need stirring. The chatter of students filled the room — chairs scraping, trays clinking, people laughing too loudly.
Yunnie was talking about something again. She was always talking, always smiling, always surrounded by people.
Jess liked that about her.
It made things easier.
Because Jess had never been good at talking.
“Jess? Are you even listening?” Yunnie laughed.
Jess blinked and smiled nervously.
“S-sorry… what did you say?”
Yunnie sighed dramatically.
“I said there’s a new transfer student today.”
Jess nodded quietly.
Transfers happened sometimes. They usually disappeared into the background after a week.
But then the cafeteria doors opened.
And someone walked in.
The girl moved slowly, like the noise didn’t belong to her world.
She had a calm face, almost unreadable, and her eyes scanned the cafeteria like she was observing something rather than participating in it.
She walked straight to the counter.
“Iced coffee with milk,” she said.
Her voice was steady.
“Little ice. Seventy-five percent sweetness.”
Jess noticed the way she ordered. So specific. So confident.
Yunnie noticed too.
“Ooh,” Yunnie murmured. “She’s interesting.”
Jess watched as the girl took her drink and turned around.
For a moment their eyes met.
Jess quickly looked away.
A few minutes later, Yunnie suddenly stood up.
“Wait here.”
Jess watched her walk toward the girl.
Yunnie talked to everyone easily. Even strangers.
Jess lowered her head slightly, pretending to check her phone while sneaking glances.
The mysterious girl looked surprised at first.
But then she smiled.
A small smile.
They talked.
Then Yunnie waved excitedly.
“Jess! Come here!”
Jess froze.
She hated meeting new people.
But she stood anyway and walked over.
“This is Linear,” Yunnie said brightly.
The girl nodded slightly.
“Hi.”
Jess gave a small bow of her head.
“H-hi.”
Linear’s eyes were calm. Almost too calm.
It felt like she was observing Jess carefully.
Jess couldn’t explain why, but it made her a little nervous.

Days passed.
Then weeks.
And somehow Linear became part of their group.
But something was different.
Yunnie laughed more when Linear was around.
They sat together often.
Sometimes Jess would arrive and see them already deep in conversation.
Yunnie seemed so comfortable with Linear.
Too comfortable.
Jess tried to ignore the feeling in her chest.
It wasn’t jealousy.
It couldn’t be.
After all, Linear was nice.
She helped Jess with homework sometimes.
She listened patiently when Jess spoke.
But there was still a strange invisible wall between them.
A distance Jess couldn’t cross.

Zen noticed Linear too.
Jess realized it the day they were studying together.
Zen leaned back in his chair, watching Linear explain something on the whiteboard.
She spoke clearly.
Confidently.
Even the teacher nodded.
Zen smirked.
“Smart,” he murmured.
Jess felt something twist inside her.
Zen was her boyfriend.
But sometimes it felt like he was just… playing the role.
Still, she loved him.
Even if he didn’t say it back often.

Dan was different.
Quiet.
Gentle.
He didn’t talk much but he always listened.
Sometimes Jess noticed him watching everyone silently.
Especially Linear.
But she couldn’t tell what he was thinking.
Dan was impossible to read.

Then came the mountain trip.
It was supposed to be fun.
Fresh air.
Campfire.
Friends together.
Jess was excited.
She thought maybe this trip would make everything feel normal again.
Maybe she could talk to Zen more.
Maybe she could talk to Yunnie like before.
Maybe the strange distance around Linear would disappear.
But the mountains were quiet.
Too quiet.
The sun was setting when it happened.
Footsteps.
Shouting.
Three men appeared from the trees.
“Hands up!”
Jess froze.
A gun.
One of them had a gun.
Her heart started pounding so loudly she could barely hear anything else.
Yunnie grabbed Jess’s hand.
Zen stepped slightly forward.
Dan stood still.
Linear didn’t move.
The robber pointed the gun.
“Phones. Money. Now.”
Everyone obeyed quickly.
Jess’s hands trembled as she handed over her bag.
It should have ended there.
But the robber looked nervous.
His hand shook.
“Don’t look at me!”
The gun suddenly pointed toward them.
Jess felt her chest tighten.
Everything slowed.
Someone moved.
Someone shouted.
Then—
BANG.
Pain exploded in Jess’s body.
She fell.
The ground felt cold.
Her vision blurred.
Next to her, Linear collapsed too.
Jess could hear people shouting.
Zen’s voice.
“Linear!”
Footsteps running.
Someone lifting Linear.
Jess tried to speak.
Her mouth wouldn’t work.
Zen.
She wanted to call him.
But he didn’t come.
Her eyes searched desperately.
She saw Zen carrying Linear away.
Running.
Getting help.
Jess tried to reach out.
Her hand barely moved.
The sky above the mountain was darkening.
Jess felt strangely calm.
Maybe Zen would come back.
He would.
He had to.
After all…
He was her boyfriend.
Right?
Her vision faded slowly.
The last thing Jess heard was the wind through the trees.
Then everything went quiet.

## II. Arc 2 — Yunnie

People always thought Yunnie had the perfect life.
She was friendly.
Confident.
Liked by everyone.
Teachers trusted her.
Classmates wanted to sit with her.
People laughed when she joked.
It was easy.
All she had to do was smile.
But smiles were easy when they didn’t mean anything.
At home, nobody really talked.
Her father worked late every night.
Her mother filled the silence with criticism.
“Why is your grade not higher?”
“Why are you always out with friends?”
“Why can’t you be more serious?”
Warmth didn’t exist in that house.
At some point, Yunnie stopped trying.
Instead, she learned how to become someone everyone liked.
If people liked you, they wouldn’t look too closely.
Jess was the closest person she had.
Jess was gentle. Loyal. Always there.
But even Jess never saw the real Yunnie.
Because Yunnie never showed her.
Some doors stayed closed.

The day Linear arrived, the cafeteria was loud like usual.
Yunnie sat across from Jess, talking about nothing in particular.
Jess was stirring her drink again.
She always did that when she was nervous.
Yunnie smiled.
Jess was sweet.
But simple.
Sometimes too simple.
Then the cafeteria doors opened.
A girl walked in.
She didn’t look around nervously like most new students.
She observed.
Like she was studying the room.
Yunnie leaned slightly forward.
Interesting.
The girl went to the counter.
“Iced coffee with milk. Little ice. Seventy-five percent sweetness.”
Her voice was calm.
Precise.
Most people stumbled when ordering in a crowded cafeteria.
She didn’t.
Yunnie stood up.
“Wait here,” she told Jess.
Jess nodded quietly.
Approaching strangers was easy for Yunnie.
She had done it hundreds of times.
“New student?” Yunnie asked.
The girl turned.
Their eyes met.
For a moment, Yunnie felt something strange.
Like the girl could see through her smile.
“Yes,” the girl said.
“I’m Linear.”

They talked.
At first it was normal conversation.
Where are you from?
Which class are you in?
But then Linear said something strange.
“You seem tired.”
Yunnie blinked.
“What?”
“You smile a lot,” Linear continued calmly. “But your eyes look tired.”
Yunnie laughed automatically.
Nobody had ever said that before.
“Wow,” she joked. “You figured me out already?”
But Linear didn’t laugh.
Instead she said quietly,
“People who grow up without warmth learn to become warm for others.”
Yunnie’s smile froze.
For the first time in years, she didn’t know what to say.

After that day, everything changed.
Linear became part of their group quickly.
Jess liked her.
Zen noticed her intelligence.
Dan watched quietly as usual.
But Linear and Yunnie became something else.
They talked for hours.
About things Yunnie had never told anyone.
Her family.
Her loneliness.
Her anger.
Linear always listened carefully.
Then she gave advice.
Simple advice.
“Try speaking to your father when he isn’t busy.”
“Your mother criticizes because that’s the only way she knows how to communicate.”
“Don’t fight their behavior. Change the direction of the conversation.”
Slowly, things at home changed.
Not dramatically.
But enough.
For the first time, Yunnie felt like someone understood her.
Jess noticed the change too.
Yunnie could tell.
Sometimes Jess looked at them strangely.
Like she wanted to join their conversations but didn’t know how.
Yunnie felt a little guilty.
But she couldn’t stop.
Being around Linear felt…
Comfortable.
Like standing next to someone who already knew every part of you.

The mountain trip was supposed to be relaxing.
Fresh air.
Friends.
Yunnie needed that.
But the mountains felt uneasy that evening.
The air was too quiet.
Then the robbers appeared.
Everything happened too fast.
Shouting.
Fear.
The gun.
Yunnie felt her body freeze.
She couldn’t move.
Then the gunshot exploded through the forest.
Jess fell.
Linear fell too.
Yunnie screamed.
Zen ran toward Linear immediately.
Dan grabbed Yunnie before she collapsed.
The world spun.
Darkness swallowed everything.

When Yunnie woke up, the hospital room smelled like antiseptic.
Dan sat beside her bed.
His face looked pale.
“You fainted,” he said softly.
Yunnie sat up slowly.
Her head hurt.
“Where is everyone?”
Dan hesitated.
Jess is dead.
The words hit her like ice.
Yunnie stared at him.
“No.”
Dan looked down.
“They couldn’t save her.”
Silence filled the room.
Then a question escaped Yunnie’s lips before she could stop it.
“Why did you help me?”
Dan looked confused.
“You fainted.”
“Jess needed help more than me.”
Dan didn’t answer immediately.
His fingers tightened slightly.
“I… didn’t think.”
But something in his voice felt wrong.

The days after the accident felt strange.
The group had broken apart.
Zen stopped smiling.
Linear recovered quietly.
But Dan…
Dan changed.
Yunnie noticed it first.
He was restless.
Always watching.
Like someone waiting for something terrible to happen.
Even during the trip, before the robbery, he had seemed uneasy.
Now it was worse.
His eyes followed Zen constantly.
At first, Yunnie thought he was worried about him.
But then she started hearing things.
Small comments.
Sharp words.
Dan began talking to Zen privately.
Again and again.
“You left her.”
“You chose.”
“You could have saved her.”
Zen tried to ignore him.
But Dan didn’t stop.
Every day.
Every moment.
Slowly.
Carefully.
Dan pushed.
Like someone pressing on a bruise again and again.
Zen’s confidence disappeared.
His jokes stopped.
His eyes looked hollow.
Yunnie tried to intervene once.
“Dan… maybe you should stop.”
Dan looked at her calmly.
“Truth shouldn’t be comfortable.”

A month later, Zen didn’t come to school.
The news spread quickly.
Zen was dead.
Suicide.
Yunnie sat silently when she heard.
Her mind replayed everything.
Jess dying.
Dan whispering.
Zen breaking.
Something didn’t feel right.
Dan sat in the back of the classroom, staring at the window.
His face was calm again.
Too calm.
Like the storm inside him had passed.
For the first time, Yunnie felt a chill run down her spine.
Dan had never been the same since the mountain.
And somehow…
She felt like he had been waiting for something.
Something that had finally happened.

## III. Arc 3 — Zen

The cafeteria was loud.
It always had been.
Zen sat lazily in his chair, spinning a pen between his fingers while half-listening to Yunnie talk. Jess sat quietly beside her, nodding at everything Yunnie said.
It was always like this.
Yunnie filled the air.
Jess filled the silence.
Zen leaned back.
Life had always been easy for him.
His parents were famous scientists. Their research on mysterious forces and unknown phenomena had made them respected across the country.
From childhood, Zen grew up surrounded by admiration.
Teachers praised him.
Students envied him.
And he deserved it.
He was smart.
He was confident.
He was always at the top of the class.
Even debates bored him sometimes because nobody could challenge him.
Then the cafeteria door opened.
A girl walked in.
She didn’t look around nervously like new students usually did.
She simply observed.
Zen noticed her immediately.
Interesting.
The girl approached the counter.
“Iced coffee with milk. Little ice. Seventy-five percent sweetness.”
Her voice was calm.
Zen smirked slightly.
Precise people were usually intelligent.
Yunnie stood up.
“Wait here,” she told Jess before walking over to the girl.
Zen watched them talk.
Then Jess joined them.
Soon Yunnie waved at him and Dan.
“Come meet our new friend!”
Zen walked over casually.
“This is Linear,” Yunnie said.
Linear nodded slightly.
Zen studied her.
Her eyes were calm.
Not shy.
Not impressed.
Just calm.
For the first time in a long while, Zen felt curious.

He realized how different she was during their first debate.
The topic was controversial.
Most people avoided it.
Zen loved topics like that.
They showed who could actually think.
But when he finished explaining his argument, Linear tilted her head slightly.
“That logic has a flaw.”
Zen laughed.
“Oh?”
She calmly dismantled his entire argument in less than two minutes.
Not emotionally.
Logically.
Zen stared at her.
Then he smiled.
Finally.
Someone interesting.

After that, he kept talking to her.
Debates became frequent.
Sometimes they lasted hours.
Linear didn’t care about people’s opinions.
She spoke honestly.
Confidently.
It fascinated him.
He began noticing her more.
The way she thought.
The way she didn’t try to impress anyone.
The way she could stand beside him as an equal.
Zen knew that was why Yunnie liked her too.
But somewhere along the way…
Zen started liking her for another reason.
He just didn’t admit it.
Jess noticed.
Of course she did.
Jess always noticed.
But she never said anything.
That made Zen feel strangely guilty.
Yet he couldn’t stop looking at Linear.

One afternoon the group decided to plan a trip.
“To the seaside!” Yunnie suggested.
Everyone liked the idea.
Everyone except Dan.
Zen noticed immediately.
Dan was his closest friend.
Quiet.
Gentle.
Reliable.
But recently Dan had started acting strangely.
He often looked distracted.
Sometimes he seemed lost in thought.
Like he was somewhere else entirely.
A week before the trip, Dan approached him.
“We shouldn’t go.”
Zen raised an eyebrow.
“Why?”
Dan hesitated.
“It… isn’t safe.”
Zen laughed.
“Since when are you paranoid?”
Dan lowered his gaze.
“I changed it many times…”
His voice was so quiet Zen almost didn’t hear it.
“But why is the outcome the same?”
Zen frowned.
“What?”
Dan didn’t explain.

The day of the trip arrived.
The sea was beautiful.
Waves rolling slowly onto the sand.
Jess and Linear went into the water together.
Jess couldn’t swim well, so they stayed in the shallow area.
Yunnie relaxed near the cottage.
Dan and Zen watched the two girls playing in the water.
Then everything changed.
Jess stepped slightly deeper.
A strong wave crashed toward her.
It dragged her suddenly into deeper water.
Jess screamed.
Linear shouted for help and rushed toward her.
But Linear wasn’t a strong swimmer either.
Zen ran immediately.
He reached Jess first.
He pulled her out of the water and dragged her onto the sand.
“Jess! Jess!”
He began CPR.
One breath.
Two breaths.
Water spilled from Jess’s mouth.
She coughed.
Alive.
Zen felt relief flood through him.
Then he realized something.
“Where’s Linear?”
The ocean stretched endlessly.
There was no sign of her.

Behind him, chaos unfolded.
Yunnie had tried to run toward them.
But she slipped on a rock near the cottage.
Her head struck the stone.
She collapsed.
Dan saw it happen.
He hesitated only a second before turning back toward the ocean.
But suddenly the weakened cottage structure collapsed partially onto Yunnie.
Dan ran toward her immediately.
But it was too late.
Yunnie was already dead.

Three days later, the coast guard stopped searching.
Linear was declared missing.
Presumed dead.
Zen felt like his chest had been hollowed out.
Jess survived.
But Linear was gone.
Dan blamed him.
“Why didn’t you notice?”
Zen shouted back.
“You told us not to go!”
“You didn’t listen!”
“You didn’t explain!”
Their arguments grew worse every day.
Eventually they stopped speaking.
Zen couldn’t escape the guilt.
Two people were dead.
Because of him.
One night, he made a decision.
If he disappeared…
Maybe the guilt would disappear too.

Then—
Zen opened his eyes.
The cafeteria.
The same noise.
The same table.
Yunnie talking.
Jess stirring her drink.
Zen stared around in shock.
This was impossible.
Then the cafeteria doors opened.
A girl walked in.
“Iced coffee with milk. Little ice. Seventy-five percent sweetness.”
Linear.
Alive.
Zen’s chest tightened painfully.
Relief.
Shock.
Nostalgia.
From that moment on, he couldn’t stop watching her.
Jess noticed.
He knew she did.
But he couldn’t help it.
Because he remembered losing her.

Then one day someone suggested a trip again.
“To the seaside!”
Zen froze.
“No.”
Everyone looked at him.
“We shouldn’t go.”
Before anyone could argue, Dan suddenly spoke.
“If not the sea, we could go camping in the mountains.”
Zen stared at him.
Something was wrong.
Dan from before would never suggest another dangerous trip.
But Zen thought maybe it was fine.
After all…
They weren’t going to the sea anymore.
History had changed.
Or so he believed.

The robbery in the mountains proved him wrong.
When the gun fired, both Jess and Linear fell.
For a split second Zen remembered the sea.
The moment he lost Linear.
This time he grabbed her first.
He carried her away to get help.
Jess died.
Later, Zen confronted Dan.
“Why did you help Yunnie instead of Jess?”
Dan looked at him calmly.
“For the same reason you helped Linear.”
Zen felt his blood run cold.
“You remember too.”
Dan nodded slowly.
“Then why agree to the trip?”
Dan looked away.
“You wouldn’t understand.”
No matter how much Zen demanded answers, Dan stayed silent.
Instead, he began whispering things again.
“You knew the future.”
“You still couldn’t change it.”
“People died because of you again.”
The guilt crushed Zen slowly.
Even knowing the future…
He couldn’t save everyone.
Maybe fate couldn’t be changed.
Maybe everything would always end in tragedy.
Eventually the pressure became unbearable.
Zen made the same decision again.

The world faded into darkness.

## IV. Arc 4 — Dan (Part 1)

Dan opened his eyes.
White ceiling.
For a few seconds, he simply stared at it without moving.
Then slowly he turned his head toward the clock.
12:00 AM.
Exactly midnight.
Dan smirked faintly.
“Same as always… twelve o’clock. March twenty-first.”
Four months after Linear transferred to their school.
Two weeks before the planned trip.
He already knew the date without looking.
Because this wasn’t the first time.
Dan had lost count of how many times he had woken up in this room.
Always the same night.
Always the same moment.
The beginning of the loop.

Dan had always been a quiet person.
Most people thought he lived inside his own world.
But he still had friends.
Zen.
Jess.
Yunnie.
To someone introverted like him, those few people meant everything.
They were his entire world.
Then one day Linear appeared.
At first he didn’t think much of her.
She seemed calm.
Interesting.
But slowly he noticed something.
Yunnie became closer to Linear.
Zen started paying attention to her.
Jess looked more unhappy every day.
Dan didn’t like it.
Not because he hated Linear.
But because he felt something changing inside their group.
Something unstable.
Still, he accepted it.
That was how relationships worked.
People changed.
Groups changed.
Until the first tragedy.

The first trip they ever planned was to Angkor Wat in Siem Reap.
Everyone was excited.
Dan remembered their laughter clearly.
The night everything ended.
They went clubbing.
Music.
Alcohol.
Dancing.
Eventually Zen and Dan went to the bathroom together, leaving the three girls at the table.
It was only a few minutes.
Just a few minutes.
But when they returned—
The girls were gone.
At first they thought they had simply gone somewhere else.
But they never came back.
The search lasted hours.
Then days.
When the police finally found them…
Dan wished they hadn’t.
Jess.
Yunnie.
Linear.
All three had been kidnapped, raped, and murdered.
The images destroyed him.
Zen was the same.
They both drowned in guilt.
Dan decided he couldn’t live anymore.
He planned to kill himself quietly.
But Zen found out.
Zen tried to stop him.
During the struggle, the knife slipped.
It pierced Zen’s chest.
Dan still remembered the exact sound Zen made.
A soft gasp.
Then silence.
Blood spread across the floor.
Dan stared at his friend’s body.
His mind broke.
So he smiled.
And killed himself too.

Then he woke up.
In this room.
On this bed.
At 12:00 AM, March 21st.
The world had reset.
That was when Dan swore an oath.
He would stop the tragedy.

When the group suggested visiting Siem Reap again, he rejected it immediately.
“No.”
His voice was unusually firm.
So they changed the destination.
The seaside.
Dan hesitated.
Maybe if the location changed…
Maybe the future would change.
Before the trip he warned Zen.
“This trip is dangerous.”
Zen laughed.
“You’re overthinking.”
And the tragedy still came.
At the beach, Jess survived.
But Linear disappeared.
And Yunnie died.
Another broken ending.
Dan lost his mind again.
This time he blamed Zen.
Relentlessly.
Until Zen couldn’t bear it.
Zen committed suicide.
Dan ran to the scene.
Jess was crying beside Zen’s body.
Dan picked up the knife.
But Jess stopped him.
She hugged him tightly.
“Stop.”
That was the last thing he remembered.

Then he woke up again.
Same room.
Same bed.
Same time.
The loop continued.

The next time he refused every trip.
No traveling.
No leaving school.
Eventually everyone returned home for break.
Except Linear and Zen, who waited for their parents.
Jess, Yunnie, and Dan boarded a bus to Phnom Penh.
Halfway through the journey—
Another bus crashed directly into them.
Metal screamed.
Glass shattered.
Dan saw his friends die in front of him.
With his final breath he wondered one thing.
What did I do wrong?
Then darkness.
And again—
He woke up.

This time he broke.
Dan cried uncontrollably in his room.
No matter what he changed…
Someone always died.
So he became paranoid.
Nobody could leave.
Nobody could travel.
He followed his friends everywhere.
Jess and Yunnie felt uncomfortable.
But Linear calmed them.
“Maybe Dan is just stressed after exams,” she said gently.
“Let him be.”

Eventually the group insisted on having a party in their dorm room.
They couldn’t travel.
They couldn’t go home.
At least they could celebrate together.
Dan didn’t like it.
But he couldn’t stop them.
They grilled meat.
Drank alcohol.
Jess became very drunk.
Then everything fell apart.
Jess suddenly exploded.
She confronted Yunnie.
“Why are you so close to Linear?!”
Her voice shook with resentment.
“I was your friend first!”
Yunnie, also drunk, felt attacked.
They began arguing loudly.
Linear looked uncomfortable.
Then Jess turned toward Zen.
Her accusations made the room even more awkward.
Finally Jess stormed out.
Yunnie followed her.
Zen followed them both.
Dan started to go too—
But he hesitated.
He didn’t want to leave Linear alone.
So he sat beside her.
Neither of them spoke for a moment.
Then Linear asked quietly,
“Why didn’t you follow them?”
“I’ll keep you company.”
“Why?”
Dan answered simply.
“I don’t want you to be alone.”
Linear smiled slightly.
“I’m fine.”
She looked directly at him.
“I won’t die.”
Dan’s expression darkened.
“You have no idea.”
Linear suddenly smiled brightly.
“Have you ever seen me die with your own eyes?”
Something clicked in Dan’s mind.
She was right.
In every timeline—
Linear was either missing.
Or already dead when he found her.
He had never seen her die himself.
Was Linear the key to the loop?
Before he could think further—
A loud noise echoed outside.
They ran out.
And froze.
Jess and Yunnie lay on the ground below the school building.
Broken.
Dead.
Zen stood on the rooftop above.
His hand still stretched outward.
Dan collapsed to his knees.
Why…
Why can’t I change anything?
He closed his eyes.
Waiting for the reset.
But it never came.

Three months passed.
Life moved forward for the first time.
Dan questioned everything.
Why hadn’t the loop restarted?
Zen eventually explained what happened that night.
Jess was drunk and emotional.
She ran to the rooftop.
Yunnie followed her.
They argued.
Jess confessed she hated Linear.
Yunnie slapped her.
Jess slipped.
Yunnie tried to catch her.
They both fell.
Zen arrived too late.
Dan felt empty.
Nothing made sense anymore.

Months later, Zen confessed something to Dan.
“I think I like Linear.”
Dan exploded.
“Because of her, Yunnie and Jess died!”
Zen shook his head.
“That’s not her fault.”
They argued.
And Linear heard everything.
She walked away silently.
After that she avoided them.
Zen’s mental state collapsed again.
One day he stopped Linear to talk.
But she looked at him with cold eyes.
“I wish the one who died that day was you… not Yunnie and Jess.”
The words destroyed him.
Zen stood motionless.
Dan watched from far away.
Then suddenly—
Zen ran.
Dan realized too late where he was going.
The rooftop.
The same building.
“STOP!”
But Zen had already jumped.
His body hit the ground.
Dead.
Everything faded.
Dan opened his eyes again.
Back in his room.
March 21st.
12:00 AM.
That was when he finally understood.
Zen’s death…
was the trigger for the regression.

## V. Arc 5 — Dan (Part 2)

Dan opened his eyes.
He didn’t move.
He stared at the white ceiling above him.
No tears.
No pain.
Only calm.
At this point, he already knew something.
No matter what he changed…
No matter what decision he made…
The ending always returned to tragedy.
So instead of trying to save everyone again, he decided something different.
If he couldn’t stop the ending…
Then he would find the reason for the loop.
And break it.

Dan began replaying every timeline he could remember.
Jess.
Yunnie.
Zen.
None of them ever remembered the loops.
But what about Linear?
Sometimes she said strange things…
But that didn’t necessarily mean anything.
Maybe she was just comforting him when he looked uneasy.
Still… something about her felt different.
Special.
But he couldn’t explain why.

The next morning, Dan walked toward the cafeteria.
Before he reached it, he noticed someone sitting alone on a bench.
It was Linear.
She sat under the sunlight quietly, looking at a field of flowers across the courtyard.
Dan walked over and sat beside her.
For a moment, neither of them spoke.
Then he asked casually,
“Are those flowers beautiful?”
Linear hummed softly.
“Hmm.”
Dan looked at them again.
“Do you like them?”
Linear shook her head slightly.
“I’m sitting here because I like the sunlight.”
She stretched her hand slightly into the warm light.
“I like the warmth of sunlight. I can’t get enough of it.”
Dan laughed lightly.
“Most people try to avoid sunlight. But not you.”
Linear kept looking forward.
“I used to live in a place that had no sunlight for a very long time.”
Then she slowly turned her head and looked straight into Dan’s eyes.
“So I really yearn for it.”
Dan smirked and teased her.
“You know sunlight can give you skin cancer, right? That could make you die pretty quickly.”
Linear answered calmly.
“Cancer isn’t as painful as drowning.”
She paused.
“Or being raped… and then killed.”
Dan’s smile froze instantly.
“…What did you say?”
Linear tilted her head slightly.
“Hm?”
She smiled innocently.
Dan stared at her in shock.
“How do you know that?”
He shook his head quickly.
“No… that’s not the right question.”
His voice became serious.
“Who are you exactly… Linear?”
Linear looked at him calmly.
“If I tell you I’m a witch… would you believe me?”
Dan took a slow breath, trying to calm himself.
“Then explain something to me.”
His eyes sharpened.
“Why are you trapping us in this loop?”
“Linear… what do you want?”
Linear shook her head gently.
“I didn’t trap you in this loop.”
She looked at him quietly.
“You trapped yourself.”
Dan frowned.
“…How?”
Linear spoke softly.
“This loop should have ended a long time ago.”
“But your obsession keeps it alive.”
Dan’s mind started racing.
“I don’t understand.”
Linear continued calmly.
“Someone once taught me how to drink coffee.”
“If the sweetness is 0%, it’s too bitter.”
“If it’s 100%, it’s too sweet.”
Dan muttered,
“So… 50/50?”
Linear shook her head.
“No.”
“Because then you can’t tell whether it’s sweet or bitter.”
She smiled faintly.
“75% is the best.”
“You can taste the sweetness…”
“But the bitterness is still there to give it flavor.”
She looked directly into Dan’s eyes.
“Humans are the same.”
Dan’s brain went blank.
“…I don’t understand.”
“What does that have to do with my obsession?”
Linear stood up slowly.
Then she stepped in front of him.
She leaned slightly closer and asked calmly,
“Dan.”
“Do you think humans are purely evil?”
Dan said nothing.
After a moment, she spoke again.
“Or purely good?”
Silence.
Dan looked completely lost.
So Linear asked again.
“Let me ask differently.”
She looked directly into his eyes.
“Dan… do you believe you are a good person?”
Dan couldn’t answer.
His mind was filled with too many memories.
Too many deaths.
Too many failures.
He just sat there silently.
Linear straightened up.
“Maybe you need time to process this.”
She turned and began walking away.
“If you figure it out…”
“You can come find me.”
Dan remained sitting on the bench.
Watching her back slowly disappear in the sunlight.
His mind was completely blank.

## VI. Arc — Linear (Escape)

Darkness.
The tunnel stretched endlessly forward.
A girl ran through it.
Her white dress had long stopped being white.
It was soaked with dirt, dried blood, and the stains of wounds that never had time to heal.
Her bare feet slipped on the cold floor.
Still she ran.
Her breathing was broken, shallow, like every breath was cutting her lungs open.
She couldn’t see anything.
The tunnel was completely black.
She slammed into the wall.
The rough stone tore open the skin on her shoulder.
She didn’t stop.
She ran again.
And again.
Because she knew.
If she slowed down…
If she stopped even for a second…
They would catch her.
And when they caught her—
Cold metal.
Bright white lights.
Leather straps cutting into her wrists.
Scalpels.
Screams.
Her screams.
Her legs moved faster.
Her body was already beyond exhaustion.
But fear pushed her forward.
Run.
Run.
Run.
She only remembered one thing.
Sunlight.
For centuries she had dreamed about sunlight.
Warm.
Soft.
Alive.
She wanted to see it again.
Just once.

The escape only lasted forty minutes.
But to her…
It felt longer than the four hundred years she had spent inside that laboratory.
Her legs trembled.
Her vision blurred.
Then—
A faint glow appeared ahead.
At first she thought it was another illusion.
But the light grew brighter.
Warmer.
Her heart began pounding violently.
She stumbled forward.
Then suddenly—
The tunnel ended.
And sunlight exploded across her vision.
She collapsed to her knees.
The warmth touched her skin.
Soft.
Gentle.
Real.
For the first time in four hundred years, she saw the sun again.
The warmth she had begged for.
The warmth she had cried for.
The warmth she had almost forgotten.
Her fingers slowly touched the ground.
Warm dirt.
Real earth.
Not cold steel.
Not stone walls.
She began to laugh.
A broken, trembling laugh.
This girl was Linear.
And she was a witch.

Long ago, witches had lived quietly among humans.
But humans discovered them.
And humans were curious creatures.
They wanted witch abilities.
Their magic.
Their long lifespan.
So they hunted them.
Captured them.
And turned them into experiments.
When Linear was first imprisoned, there had been hundreds of witches.
Children.
Adults.
Elders.
All locked in the underground laboratory.
Four hundred years later…
Only a few dozen remained alive.
The rest had died on surgery tables.
Their bodies cut open again and again.
Their screams echoing through the corridors.
Linear remembered every single one.
She remembered the smell of blood.
The sound of bones breaking.
The feeling of being alive when she should have died long ago.
Hatred filled her chest.
A hatred so deep it had kept her alive all these centuries.
She had sworn something long ago.
If she ever escaped…
If she ever regained her strength…
She would destroy humanity.
Every last one of them.

So she ran.
Even after escaping the tunnel.
Even after reaching the surface.
Her legs could barely hold her weight anymore.
But she kept moving.
Farther.
Farther.
Far away from the place that had stolen four hundred years of her life.
The sky slowly turned dark.
Night fell.
In the distance, small lights flickered.
Human houses.
Her body swayed.
Her vision spun.
Then suddenly—
Her knees gave out.
She collapsed in the middle of a dirt road.

Rain woke her.
Cold drops hit her face.
Her body was soaked within seconds.
But she didn’t move.
She simply lay there.
Looking up at the dark sky.
Then something inside her broke.
She began laughing.
Loud.
Unstable.
Then the laughter turned into sobbing.
Four hundred years.
Four hundred years of pain.
Of humiliation.
Of rage.
She screamed into the rain.
But the sound disappeared into the night.
Then—
A motorbike approached.
Its headlight cut through the rain.
Linear didn’t move.
Instead, her fingers slowly curled.
Magic gathered weakly in her palm.
When the rider came closer…
She would kill him.
Just one human.
Just one.
To release the hatred burning inside her chest.
The motorbike got closer.
Closer.
Then suddenly—
CRASH!
The bike slipped and fell.
A man’s voice shouted angrily.
“Damn it! Who leaves something in the road like—”
His voice stopped.
The old man stared at the girl lying in the rain.
“…What the hell…”
His voice turned panicked.
“Hey! Hey! Are you alive?!”
Linear couldn’t understand what he was saying.
Her vision was fading again.
But she felt something strange.
Warm arms lifting her carefully.
The old man carried her like something fragile.
And the darkness swallowed her again.

When she woke up again…
She was inside a hospital room.
Old white walls.
Paint peeling with age.
The smell of medicine filled the air.
The old man stood beside her bed.
“She’s awake!” he shouted toward the hallway.
A nurse rushed in.
They checked her injuries.
Bandaged her wounds.
Linear glared at them with pure hatred.
But she was too weak to move.
So she waited.
Recovered slowly.
And secretly prepared something.
A curse.
A massive one.
A curse born from centuries of suffering.
A curse that would spread across humanity like a plague.
But such magic required a price.
Her life.
Linear didn’t care.
If humanity died with her…
It would be enough.

The night she finished preparing the curse…
She activated it.
A dark smile slowly appeared on her lips.
Then—
The door opened.
The old man walked in.
He carried a tray.
Food.
And a glass of iced coffee.
He laughed casually.
“You should eat before sleeping.”
He placed the tray beside her.
“I know coffee at night isn’t healthy…”
“But it helps calm the mind.”
He forced her to eat.
Then pushed the coffee toward her.
“Try it.”
Linear hesitated.
Then drank it.
She frowned slightly.
The old man laughed loudly.
“Hahaha! How is it?”
“It’s not too sweet, right?”
“But still a little bitter.”
“That’s the correct way to drink coffee.”
“If you add too much sugar, it becomes too sweet.”
“You can’t taste the bitterness anymore.”
“If there’s no sugar, it’s just bitter.”
“Fifty-fifty doesn’t work either.”
“The bitterness overwhelms the sweetness.”
He raised a finger.
“So seventy-five percent sweetness is best.”
“You taste the sweetness…”
“But the bitterness is still there.”
“Just enough to give it flavor.”
Then he added,
“And don’t add too much ice.”
“It ruins the coffee.”
“But without ice, it feels dull.”
“So a little ice is best.”
He smiled.
“Understand?”
Linear stared at him coldly.
“Why did you help me?”
The old man sighed quietly.
“Coffee is like humans.”
“No one is completely good.”
“No one is completely evil.”
“We’re mixed.”
“About seventy-five and twenty-five.”
Then he spoke about his past.
About his wife.
His job.
The stress that slowly destroyed his family.
His wife had miscarried.
And died.
“If my daughter lived,” he said quietly,
“She’d be around your age.”
He looked out the window.
“I used to hate my boss.”
“But later I realized… his boss was crushing him too.”
“That’s when I understood.”
“Nothing in this world is completely black or white.”
He looked back at her.
“So we should try to keep the sweetness larger than the bitterness.”
He smiled gently.
“That’s why I helped you.”
“I want my coffee to stay seventy-five percent sweet.”

Linear looked down at the coffee in her hands.
Neither of them spoke.
Minutes passed.
Then the old man stood up.
“Alright.”
“Rest well.”
“You can leave tomorrow.”
He paused at the door.
“Maybe you should contact your family.”
Then he left.

Moments later—
A scream echoed outside.
Doctors running.
Panic.
The old man had collapsed.
Dead.
Because of her curse.
Linear stared at the coffee.
Her hands trembled slightly.
One tear fell into the glass.
The first victim of her revenge…
Was the man who saved her life.
After a long silence—
She raised her hand.
And ended the curse.
Even though the backlash tore through her body.
That same night…
She quietly climbed out the hospital window.
And disappeared into the darkness.
